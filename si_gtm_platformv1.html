The core marketing problem
You are not fighting for attention in a crowded market. You are fighting against invisibility. A CMO at Clicks or Woolworths receives your email, it sounds interesting — and then they search "SingleInterface South Africa" and find nothing local. The deal dies not because of a bad product, but because there is no credibility signal. Every marketing activity this year should solve this problem.
What marketing IS in Year 1
  • Credibility infrastructure — articles, LinkedIn presence, panel appearances, press mentions that appear when a prospect searches your name
  • Warm-up tool for cold outreach — a prospect who has seen your content 3× before your email arrives is 4× more likely to respond
  • Reseller enablement — a deck, one-pager, and landing page that confirms a referral partner made the right introduction
  • Event content factory — every panel becomes 3 posts, a follow-up email, and a published article
What marketing is NOT in Year 1
  • Billboards, radio, mass digital display — none of these create credibility signals that close enterprise B2B deals
  • Brand campaigns — waste budget and generate vanity metrics with no pipeline traceability
  • Anything not traceable — if you can't draw a line to a conversation, a meeting, or a named prospect going further down the funnel, cut it
  • SEO/GBP framing — permanently banned across all marketing. You are an AI transformation engine for store-level growth. Full stop.
5 Core Marketing Principles
01
Never lead with the company. Always lead with the problem.Every piece of content, every ad, every press release should lead with the consequence for the brand — not with who you are. "Retailers are losing 1 in 3 local purchase decisions to AI search" is a headline. "SingleInterface launches in SA" is not.
02
One decision-maker reading something is worth a thousand impressions.An article in Supermarket & Retailer that a Shoprite CMO reads is worth more than 10,000 LinkedIn impressions from non-buyers. Think about 59 named individuals — what would each actually read, share, and remember?
03
Your personal brand IS the company brand this year.SingleInterface has no SA face other than you. Your LinkedIn, articles, panel appearances, dinner conversations — these are the brand. When you become known as "the person who understands AI and local commerce in SA", the company name follows.
04
Always create one reusable asset per activity.A CEM Africa panel becomes a LinkedIn post becomes a follow-up email becomes a published article. One dinner becomes three posts and a short report. Force multipliers, not single-use events.
05
The SEO/GBP framing is banned. Permanently.Not just in emails. Everywhere. If any marketing uses "Google Business Profile", "local SEO", or "citation management" as a headline concept — delete it.
🔵 LinkedIn — Your Primary Channel
Profile optimisation · 3×/week posting · Content types
LinkedIn is your credibility engine that works while you sleep. When a prospect checks you out after a cold email, your LinkedIn is the first thing they see. Profile optimisation should happen in Week 1.
Profile (Week 1 priority)
  • Headline: Not your job title. "Helping SA's multi-location brands grow store-by-store through AI | Country Manager, SingleInterface"
  • About: Open with the problem ("In 2027, 1 in 3 local purchase decisions begin with AI search. Most brands aren't ready.") not your CV
  • Featured: Link to best content, SI one-pager, or press mention the moment you have one
Content cadence: 3×/week
  • Type 1 — Insight post: One sharp observation about AI and local commerce. No fluff.
  • Type 2 — Proof post: A real example — client result, stat, or case study fragment
  • Type 3 — Opinion/provocation: A bold claim about your market. Invites response.
  • Rule: Never post about your company. Post about the market.

🎯 Executive Dinners — 2 Per Year
An executive dinner is a room of 10–12 CMOs or digital leaders where the host (you) facilitates a candid peer conversation about AI and local commerce. You are not presenting. You are convening. This is how you build relationships that cold emails can't.
The dinner format
  • Venue: Private dining room, top restaurant, CPT or JHB. Budget: R15–25K per dinner.
  • Guest count: 10–12. Never more. Intimacy is the product.
  • Mix: 70% prospects (senior, relevant), 30% existing contacts (credibility anchors)
  • No decks, no demos, no sales pitches during dinner. This is a peer conversation, not a vendor event.
  • Opening framing: "I'm bringing together 10 leaders to have a candid conversation about what AI is going to do to local commerce in SA over the next 24 months."
Before, during, after
  • Before: Research each guest. Personal invite (not an email blast). Set the topic expectation in the invite.
  • During: One facilitated question per course. "What's the most urgent digital problem you're trying to solve this year?" Let them talk. You listen.
  • After — within 24 hours: Personal note to each guest. No pitch. Thank them, reference something specific they said.
  • After — Day 3–5: Follow up with value only. Share an article or stat relevant to something they mentioned.
  • After — Day 7–14: This is when you ask for the meeting.

Network Activation Plan
You have three tiers of network. Each requires a different approach. The mistake most solo reps make is treating all three the same.
Tier 1 — Warm contacts
15 people
These people will take your call today. They are not a pitch list — they are a conversation list. Your job is to educate them, not sell them. Most won't buy. But 3-5 will, and the rest will introduce you.
Tier 2 — Introduceable network
50 people
You can get to these via your 15. Never cold approach a Tier 2 contact — always ask your mutual connection for a warm intro first. A warm intro has a 5x higher response rate than cold outreach.
Tier 3 — SaaS resellers
3 resellers
These are your force multipliers. Each reseller has their own network of brand relationships. If you brief them correctly and give them the right incentives, they will generate pipeline you never could solo.
Tier 1: How to work your 15 warm contacts
Do this in Week 1 — before any cold outreach
  • Call, don't email. A call takes 10 minutes and builds more trust than 10 emails. Text first if needed: "Got 10 mins this week? Want to show you something."
  • Frame it as a briefing, not a pitch. "I've just launched SingleInterface in SA and I want your honest read on whether this has legs here." People love being asked for their opinion.
  • Ask two qualifying questions: "Do you manage X locations?" and "Is your digital presence managed centrally or location-by-location?"
  • If they're a fit: Book a demo for the following week. Don't demo on the first call.
  • If they're not a fit: "Who else in your network manages multi-location brands and might have this problem?" This is where your 50-person network comes from.
  • Track every contact in your CRM: date called, what was said, next action, referral asked.
Tier 3: Reseller activation brief

Reseller Strategy

  
3 SaaS martech resellers who know your category. Activated correctly, they can generate 30-40% of your Year 1 ARR without you touching the pipeline.


  

    
Why resellers are your biggest untapped asset

    
You are one person. Your resellers each have their own client books, relationships, and credibility in the SA martech market. They are already in rooms you can't get into. The risk: if you don't brief and incentivise them properly in Month 1, they will deprioritise SI in favour of products with more support, better margins, or easier demos. Move fast on this.

  


  

    

      
What good reseller activation looks like

      
The actions that turn a reseller from passive to active

      
    
        
  • Week 1: Call each reseller. Brief them on SI positioning, pricing, and target ICP. Ask: "Which 3 of your current clients have multi-location brands with 20+ stores?"
    • 
        
  • Week 2: Send them a reseller pack — one-pager, demo video, objection handling guide, and pricing sheet. Confirm commission structure in writing.
    • 
        
  • Week 3: Run a joint demo together for their first target account. You do the demo, they manage the relationship. This is how they learn to sell it.
    • 
        
  • Month 2: Set a pipeline review rhythm — 30 min every 2 weeks. Review their active opportunities, help them with objections, and jointly strategise on deals.
    • 
        
  • Ongoing: Introduce a deal registration system — they register a prospect, you protect their commission even if the prospect comes to you directly later.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Reseller economics

      
What makes this worth their time

      
    
        
  • Recommended commission: 15-20% of Year 1 ACV. Confirm with Tarun. Without a clear number, resellers won't prioritise.
    • 
        
  • Example: reseller closes Hungry Lion (500 stores). ACV = $102,000. Their commission = $15,300-$20,400. That's a strong incentive.
    • 
        
  • Offer a kicker for the first deal — e.g. 25% commission on the first deal closed within 90 days. Creates urgency to move quickly.
    • 
        
  • Consider a co-marketing fund — offer to split the cost of a joint event or campaign. Makes SI feel like a real partner, not just another vendor in their stack.
    • 
        
  • Make renewal commissions part of the deal too — even 5% of Year 2 ACV keeps them engaged long-term.
    • 
      

    

  


  

    
Reseller red flags to watch for

    
If any of these appear, address them immediately

    

      

        
Red flag 1

        
"We're still working on understanding the product." After 30 days, any reseller who hasn't run a demo isn't going to. Have the direct conversation or move on.

      

      

        
Red flag 2

        
"We have some conversations happening." Vague pipeline language means nothing is actually moving. Demand: company name, decision maker name, and next step.

      

      

        
Red flag 3

        
"We're focused on [other product] right now." Resellers who say this in Month 2 won't change. Either sweeten the deal or accept they won't be a meaningful pipeline source.
